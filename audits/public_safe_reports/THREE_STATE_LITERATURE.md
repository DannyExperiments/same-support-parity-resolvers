# Q820 three-state supplement — literature comparison

27 November 2026. **High confidence** in the match to the source conventions and in the published comparison. **Moderate confidence** that the checked literature contains no earlier presentation of this explicit three-state example. Its priority and minimum possible state count remain undetermined. This note covers literature and scope. The director has the mathematical proof and replay audit.

## Exact question

Henzinger, Prakash and Thejaswini’s first question in §6 explicitly asks about distribution sensitivity for SR and MR, after stating support invariance for MA. Section 2 defines an almost-sure resolver by requiring probability-one acceptance of each fixed word in the nondeterministic language. The word is fixed independently of the random run. MR uses a one-memory-state resolver. The MA assertion assumes a winning memoryless strategy against a letter chooser who can observe the run. The source uses complete transition-colored parity, with at most one edge for each (source, letter, destination), and accepts when the largest infinitely repeated priority is even. Those are the supplement’s conventions. ## What the example adds

The deposited four-state proof uses two reset-survival states and two delay states. The supplement uses one delay state and an (S)-to-(R) edge, with a weighted row that maintains equal surviving masses. It retains the binary alphabet, three transition priorities, universal nondeterministic language, full support of both memoryless resolvers, one changed state/letter row, and uniformity of the failing resolver. This is a reduction from four to three states for the explicit witness within the same transition-colored model. The tagged literature note was checked directly and matches the local copy byte-for-byte. [v1.0.0 manuscript and release](https://github.com/DannyExperiments/same-support-parity-resolvers/releases/tag/v1.0.0) · [Tagged literature audit](https://github.com/DannyExperiments/same-support-parity-resolvers/blob/v1.0.0/audits/public_safe_reports/LITERATURE.md).

The qualitative negative answer is already implicit in Baier, Bertrand and Größer’s common-support, different-positive-language family and its zero–one Rabin conversion. Größer’s detailed conversion preserves common support because the outgoing probabilities are sums of products of input probabilities. The disjunctive product and a fixed parity monitor give the universal-language counterexample described in the tagged audit. That argument establishes qualitative sensitivity. The explicit three-state, single-row and uniform-reweighting constraints are additional features of the supplement.
