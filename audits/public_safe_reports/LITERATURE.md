# Q820 primary-source and scope review

**Verdict, 27 November 2026.** High confidence that the qualitative negative answer to the fixed-word support question follows from older constructions. The recovered packet's four-state automaton gives a short explicit example. The two memoryless resolvers differ at just one row and one is uniform. This review found no earlier presentation of that specific example in the checked sources. Priority and minimality of its size remain unasserted.

## Source and quantifiers

Thomas A. Henzinger, Keya Prakash and K. S. Thejaswini, *Resolving Nondeterminism with Randomness*, MFCS 2025, LIPIcs 345, article 57, DOI [10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2025.57](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2025.57), §6 first question, [proceedings p. 57:16](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol345-mfcs2025/LIPIcs.MFCS.2025.57/LIPIcs.MFCS.2025.57.pdf#page=16). The source asks whether changing the exact probabilities of an almost-sure resolver preserves its guarantee in the stochastic setting. The complete input word is fixed independently of the outcome of all random choices. The [author preprint §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12872v1#page=19) contains the same question. The source's adaptive-adversary MA-game support result concerns resolvers that are already winning with the letter chooser observing the run. That hypothesis differs from the fixed-word requirement.

## Implication of prior constructions

1. Christel Baier, Nathalie Bertrand and Marcus Größer, *Probabilistic Automata over Infinite Words: Expressiveness, Efficiency, and Decidability*, [arXiv:0907.4760, Lemmas 3.6 and 3.9 and Theorem 3.12](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.4760). Lemma 3.9 uses the same edges for every interior parameter and the languages of positive acceptance satisfy Lν ⊊ Lλ for λ < ν. Theorem 3.12 transforms each probabilistic Büchi automaton into a probabilistic Rabin automaton with acceptance probability zero or one for each word and preserves its positive language. The full construction is in Marcus Thomas Größer, *Reduction Methods for Probabilistic Model Checking*, TU Dresden dissertation, [Theorem 4.3.2 and construction, printed pp. 78–79](https://wwwtcs.inf.tu-dresden.de/ALGI/PUB/thesis_groesser.pdf). Admissibility of a successor tuple depends on supports, reachable sets and bookkeeping. An aggregate transition weight is a sum of products of input probabilities, so its positivity is constant under common-support reweighting. The transformed automata share a state set, initial state and Rabin acceptance pairs. See also Baier, Größer and Bertrand, *Probabilistic ω-Automata*, JACM 59(1), Article 1 (2012), [DOI 10.1145/2108242.2108243](https://doi.org/10.1145/2108242.2108243).
2. Make a synchronous product of two copies of that common state graph. A product state is an ordered pair of their states. In each step both copies read the same letter and sample their successors independently. A product edge has positive weight exactly when both coordinate edges do. Use the acceptance condition that the first component's Rabin condition holds or the second component's Rabin condition fails. The infrequently versus infinitely often visited component states can be determined from the projections of the infinitely often visited pairs, using finiteness. This acceptance condition is therefore Muller on the product state set.
3. Equip the product with one deterministic latest-appearance-record parity monitor. Fix its initial permutation and update it using the successor pair. Move that pair to the front. Write k for its position before the move and S for the set of the old first k pairs. Output priority 2k if S is Muller-accepting and 2k−1 otherwise. Eventually the infinitely recurring pairs occupy precisely the front |Inf| positions. Maximal recurring k equals |Inf|, so maximum-infinitely-often parity agrees with Muller acceptance. Monitor states, transitions and priorities are fixed for all probability assignments.
4. With weights (ν,ν), every fixed word is accepted with probability one. Its first coordinate almost surely accepts when the word is in Lν, and its second almost surely fails the original Rabin condition otherwise. For each fixed word a probability-one accepting event contains a run on the supported edges, so the underlying nondeterministic language equals the set of all infinite words. With weights (ν,λ), each word in Lλ ∖ Lν is almost surely rejected. The two resolvers have common support, meaning identical sets of positive transitions out of every state/letter row of the same transition-colored parity automaton.

## Nearby results and limits

[Resolving Nondeterminism by Chance, CONCUR 2025](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CONCUR.2025.32) studies thresholds and includes both finite- and infinite-word cases. [Layered Automata, LICS 2026](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.LICS.2026.24), Proposition 17, gives a qualitative probability result within its layered setting. The present example is the concrete four-state comparison and independent block proof. It also rules out uniform reweighting on the demonstrated successful full support. It makes no claim about arbitrary other supports of the same graph or a Büchi-only restriction. For a fixed ultimately periodic input, finite memory and the period yield a finite homogeneous Markov chain after the prefix. Under shared support its reachable bottom components and their maximal priorities are unchanged, so such inputs cannot separate probability-one acceptance this way.
